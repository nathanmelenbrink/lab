However, I quickly realized that many of the sensors I need to successfully and rigorously tackle this question either do not exist at all or are extortionately expensive, so I have dedicated the first year of my PhD to building them myself. Without taking any other formal engineering classes, I have designed, built, and installed a fleet of novel instruments called automated minirhizotrons that will be patented and possibly taken to enterprise, in addition to being a significant contribution to the field of ecology. I'm now working on cheaper, open-source equivalents to preexisting, commercial environmental sensors to help democratize environmental research and further my PhD. At least 1 of the 3 chapters of my PhD will be a dedicated engineering paper introducing a bespoke sensor network for monitoring plant belowground carbon allocations, including a method of using radio and/or WiFi to acquire data in real time.
